# Problem statement

## Problem
Child undernutrition is influenced by diet, illness, household conditions and access to services. Growth measurements are collected in community and school settings, but repeated measurements can be difficult to interpret consistently. This project demonstrates a lightweight way to record measurements, calculate screening indicators and highlight follow-up needs.

## Stakeholders
- Anganwadi and ASHA workers: record visits and follow-up flags.
- School nurses and health supervisors: review trends and referral prompts.
- Programme administrators: view aggregate screening patterns.
- Children and caregivers: benefit indirectly from timely professional assessment.

## Objectives
1. Capture age, sex, weight, height, MUAC and selected diet/illness indicators.
2. Calculate educational WAZ/HAZ/WHZ approximations.
3. Apply transparent classification rules and a trend-based early-risk signal.
4. Explain flagged factors and provide cautious next-step guidance.
5. Provide dashboard, child history and CSV export.

## Scope and limitations
This is a synthetic-data college prototype. It does not implement official WHO LMS tables, clinical validation, secure production authentication, offline sync, multilingual content or integration with government health systems. Thresholds and simplified equations are demonstrative and must not guide treatment.

## Indian context and potential impact
Community nutrition programmes depend on repeated growth monitoring and referral pathways. A simple digital record can illustrate how longitudinal information and clear follow-up lists might support timely review. Any real deployment would require local-language usability testing, data protection, clinical governance, validated references and approval from relevant authorities.
